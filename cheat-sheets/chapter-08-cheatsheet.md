## Chapter 8: Regulatory Landscape Cheat Sheet

### EU AI Act Risk Tiers
| Tier | Examples | Obligation |
|---|---|---|
| Unacceptable | Social scoring by public authorities, subliminal manipulation, real-time public biometric ID by law enforcement (narrow exceptions), predictive policing on profiling alone, untargeted facial scraping, workplace/education emotion recognition | **Banned** (Art. 5) |
| High-risk | Annex III: employment/HR, credit scoring, law enforcement, education, biometric categorization, critical infrastructure, migration; Annex I: safety components under existing EU product law | Risk mgmt system, data governance, technical docs, logging, human oversight, conformity assessment, EU database registration |
| Limited | Chatbots, deepfakes, AI-generated content, emotion recognition (non-workplace) | Transparency disclosure (Art. 50) only |
| Minimal | Spam filters, AI in video games | No legal obligation; voluntary codes encouraged |

### Key EU AI Act Dates
| Date | Milestone |
|---|---|
| Aug 1, 2024 | Act enters into force |
| Feb 2, 2025 | Prohibited practices enforceable |
| Aug 2, 2025 | GPAI model obligations apply |
| Aug 2, 2026 | Most Annex III high-risk obligations apply |
| Aug 2, 2027 | Annex I (regulated product) high-risk obligations apply |

### EU AI Act Fine Tiers
| Violation | Max Fine |
|---|---|
| Prohibited practices (Art. 5) | €35M or 7% global turnover |
| High-risk obligation failures | €15M or 3% global turnover |
| Incorrect info to authorities | €7.5M or 1% global turnover |

### NIST AI RMF Core Functions
| Function | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Govern | Cross-cutting foundation: policy, culture, accountability |
| Map | Establish context, identify risks for specific use case |
| Measure | Analyze/track risk quantitatively & qualitatively |
| Manage | Allocate resources to treat/respond to prioritized risks |
Voluntary, US-origin, not certifiable. Generative AI Profile = NIST AI 600-1 (2024).

### ISO/IEC 42001 (AIMS)
- Published Dec 2023; first certifiable international AI management system standard.
- Follows Annex SL high-level structure (like ISO 9001/27001): Context, Leadership, Planning, Support, Operation, Performance Evaluation, Improvement.
- Annex A controls: AI system impact assessment, lifecycle management, data governance for AI, third-party/supplier AI risk.
- Third-party accredited certification available — differentiator vs NIST AI RMF's voluntary self-assessment.

### Crosswalk Approach
Build one internal control set → map each control to specific clauses across EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, and ISO 42001 → reuse single evidence set for multi-framework audits. Avoids triplicated compliance programs.

### Conformity Assessment Routes
- **Self-assessment (Annex VI):** default for most Annex III systems if harmonized standards followed.
- **Notified body (Annex VII):** required for select high-stakes uses (e.g., remote biometric ID) or when standards not fully applied.

### Exam Traps
- Prohibited ≠ High-risk: prohibited has NO compliance pathway; high-risk does.
- Chatbots = limited risk (transparency only), NOT high-risk.
- GPAI obligations date (Aug 2025) is often confused with high-risk date (Aug 2026).
