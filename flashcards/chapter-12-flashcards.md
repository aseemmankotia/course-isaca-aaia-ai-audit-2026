## Chapter 12: Full AAIA Exam Simulation and Time-Management Strategy — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | AAIA exam question format and approximate count? | Roughly 90 scenario-based questions spanning three domains. Exam tip: expect heavy scenario weighting — pure definition recall is rare. |
| 2 | AAIA scoring scale and approximate passing score? | 200-800 scale, minimum passing score approximately 450. Verify the exact current cut score on ISACA's official site before your exam. |
| 3 | What are the three AAIA exam domains? | AI Operations, AI Governance and Risk, AI Auditing Tools and Techniques — each weighted independently; review the official exam guide for exact percentage weights. |
| 4 | Best practice: what to do with a question you're unsure about on first pass? | Make your best guess, flag it, and move on. Never leave it blank — an unanswered question is guaranteed wrong; a flagged guess still has a chance. |
| 5 | NIST AI RMF vs ISO/IEC 42001 — exam differentiator? | NIST AI RMF = voluntary risk-management guidance, not certifiable. ISO/IEC 42001 = internationally certifiable AI management system standard. Exam tests which one supports third-party certification (42001). |
| 6 | SHAP vs LIME — exam differentiator? | SHAP = game-theory-based, consistent additive attribution, both global and local. LIME = local linear surrogate approximation around one prediction, faster but less theoretically consistent. Exam scenario: 'consistent, theoretically grounded' → SHAP. |
| 7 | Process-of-elimination tip for scenario distractors? | Eliminate options containing absolutes first ('always', 'never', 'immediately disable', 'reject outright') — real-world AI governance answers are almost always risk-based and proportional, not extreme. |
| 8 | Recommended pacing check-in strategy for a ~90-question timed exam? | Do a time check at the 25%, 50%, and 75% question marks. If behind pace, switch to flag-and-move mode: answer straightforward items fast, flag hard ones for the final review pass. |
| 9 | EU AI Act risk tier structure (as tested conceptually)? | Four tiers: Unacceptable (banned), High (strict conformity requirements), Limited (transparency obligations), Minimal (no specific obligations). Exam tests matching a scenario to the correct tier. |
| 10 | Rule for changing an answer during the final review pass? | Only change an answer if you find you misread the question or scenario — do NOT change an answer just because of second-guessing/anxiety. First-instinct accuracy is usually higher. |
| 11 | Scaled score (200-800) purpose vs raw percent-correct? | Scaled scoring adjusts for statistical difficulty differences across exam forms so results are comparable across candidates who received different question sets. |
| 12 | Common distractor trap in service/framework-selection questions? | Watch for options that are 'plausible but wrong category' — e.g., a binding regulation (EU AI Act) offered as an answer to a question asking for a certifiable management standard (ISO/IEC 42001). Match the option's TYPE to what the question actually asks for. |
