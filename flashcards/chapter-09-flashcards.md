## Chapter 9: Mapping AI Governance to Auditable Controls — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Control frequency for a requirement worded 'after any material change/drift occurs'? | Event-driven/triggered frequency, not calendar-based (annual/quarterly). Exam tip: match control frequency to the exact language of the requirement. |
| 2 | Preventive vs Detective vs Corrective AI controls — one-line definitions? | Preventive = stops risk before it occurs (e.g., pipeline bias gate). Detective = identifies risk after it occurs (e.g., drift alert). Corrective = remediates after detection (e.g., automated rollback). Exam tip: classify by WHEN in the risk lifecycle the control acts. |
| 3 | KRI vs KPI — core distinction? | KRI = leading, threshold-based risk signal triggering escalation. KPI = measures achievement of a performance/process objective against a target. Exam tip: KRIs have escalation triggers; KPIs have targets. |
| 4 | Minimum elements a control objective must contain to be 'auditable'? | Owner, frequency, evidence type/artifact, defined trigger or schedule, and a measurable pass/fail criterion. Exam tip: a policy statement missing these elements is NOT yet an auditable control. |
| 5 | EU AI Act Article 72 — post-market monitoring requirement nature? | Continuous, systematic, proportionate to risk, documented in a post-market monitoring plan, across the AI system's lifecycle. Exam tip: not a one-time report. |
| 6 | ISO/IEC 42001 core management-system components tested on the exam? | AI Management System (AIMS) requiring context establishment, leadership commitment, risk-based planning, support, operation, performance evaluation (internal audit + management review), and improvement. Exam tip: mirrors ISO 27001-style management-system structure applied to AI. |
| 7 | Strongest audit evidence for a continuous control operating over a 12-month period? | Timestamped system-generated logs showing recurring execution plus evidence of investigation/closure — NOT a single screenshot or self-attestation. Exam tip: point-in-time evidence proves existence, not operating effectiveness. |
| 8 | Gap analysis prioritization principle under limited remediation resources? | Prioritize by risk exposure/severity of the gap, not by scope (number of models affected) or deadline status alone. Exam tip: absence of an incident-response capability often outranks partial-completion issues. |
| 9 | NIST AI RMF 1.0 — four core functions? | Govern, Map, Measure, Manage. Exam tip: 'Govern' is the cross-cutting function underpinning the other three; control-mapping questions often reference this function. |
| 10 | Control ownership documentation — what must be captured beyond 'who owns it'? | Owner's role/title, backup owner, escalation path, and accountable governance body. Exam tip: single-person ownership without a backup/escalation path is itself a control gap. |
| 11 | Three Lines of Defense applied to AI governance — who owns AI controls at each line? | 1st line = model/ML teams (operate controls); 2nd line = AI governance/risk function (design, monitor, challenge); 3rd line = internal audit (independent assurance). Exam tip: 2nd-line staff performing 3rd-line audit testing violates independence — a common trap. |
| 12 | When a KRI breaches its escalation threshold, what is the correct control action? | Escalate immediately to the defined governance body per the documented escalation procedure — do not wait for the next scheduled review or auto-remediate without review. Exam tip: escalation timing/procedure must be pre-defined, not ad hoc. |
