## Chapter 10: Scoping and Planning AI Audits: Control Testing Strategy — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | What are the four core control testing techniques an AI auditor uses to gather evidence? | Inquiry, Observation, Inspection, and Reperformance — listed in ascending order of evidence strength. Exam tip: reperformance is the strongest; inquiry alone never suffices for a key control. |
| 2 | Which testing technique is weakest as standalone audit evidence? | Inquiry — asking someone how a control works. Must be corroborated with observation, inspection, or reperformance for key/high-risk controls. Exam tip: 'inquiry alone' answers are usually wrong. |
| 3 | What is the primary purpose of a walkthrough during AI audit planning? | To confirm process understanding, trace data end-to-end through the pipeline, and identify key controls and risks — NOT to conclude on operating effectiveness. Exam tip: walkthrough ≠ sufficient control testing. |
| 4 | What drives audit depth allocation across an AI model inventory? | Inherent risk tiering (impact × likelihood) — high-risk models (e.g., credit, hiring, health decisions) get the deepest testing; low-risk tools get lighter procedures. Exam tip: never test all models with equal depth. |
| 5 | Why use stratified sampling for subgroup fairness/representativeness testing on training data? | It guarantees adequate coverage of small minority subgroups that simple random sampling could underrepresent or miss entirely. Exam tip: subgroup-level bias testing → stratified sampling. |
| 6 | Before relying on second-line Model Risk Management (MRM) validation work, what must Internal Audit assess? | MRM's competence, objectivity, and quality of work, then corroborate with independent sample testing. Exam tip: never rely blindly on second-line work without this assessment. |
| 7 | What must a properly documented AI audit test step include? | Control objective, testing technique, sample size/selection method, and pass/fail criteria. Exam tip: missing pass/fail criteria is a common documentation gap tested on the exam. |
| 8 | What is an appropriate source of 'audit criteria' for an AI system? | Recognized frameworks (NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001) tailored to applicable regulation and internal policy — not personal opinion or vendor self-attestation. Exam tip: criteria must be objective and agreed before fieldwork. |
| 9 | Observation vs. Inspection — key evidence difference? | Observation = watching a control performed live (point-in-time, subject to observer effect). Inspection = examining existing documentary evidence, which can cover a historical period. Exam tip: use inspection when historical operating effectiveness is needed. |
| 10 | What does 'reperformance' mean for a control like model drift monitoring? | Independently redoing the control activity (e.g., recalculating the drift metric from raw data) to confirm the original result. Highest-reliability technique. Exam tip: use for high-risk/key quantitative controls. |
| 11 | Can Internal Audit exclude a high-risk AI model from scope because a second-line function owns it? | No — IA's independent assurance role covers high-risk areas regardless of first/second-line ownership. Exam tip: scoping is driven by inherent risk, not organizational ownership. |
| 12 | What is the 'audit universe' in AI auditing? | The complete inventory of AI systems/models, ranked and tiered by inherent risk, used to build the audit plan. Exam tip: audit universe + risk tiering = foundation of scoping decisions. |
