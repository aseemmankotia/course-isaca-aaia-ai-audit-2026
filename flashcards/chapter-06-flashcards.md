## Chapter 6: AI Governance Structures, Roles, and Accountability — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Three Lines of Defense (AI): Which line owns day-to-day AI risk controls? | First line — model owners/developers/business units who build and operate the AI system. Exam tip: first line = operational ownership, not independent oversight. |
| 2 | Which line of defense sets AI risk policy and provides independent monitoring/challenge (e.g., model risk management, AI ethics board)? | Second line — independent risk/compliance function embedded in ongoing operations that sets policy and challenges first-line decisions. |
| 3 | Which line of defense provides independent assurance on the overall AI governance framework's effectiveness? | Third line — Internal Audit; performs periodic independent testing and reports to the Audit Committee/Board. |
| 4 | Chief AI Officer (CAIO) vs Model Risk Owner — key difference? | CAIO = strategic, enterprise-wide accountability for AI strategy and governance. Model Risk Owner = accountable for the risk of one SPECIFIC model/use case (first line). |
| 5 | Data Steward vs Model Risk Owner — key difference? | Data Steward owns data quality, lineage, and access controls feeding AI pipelines. Model Risk Owner owns the overall risk of the model/use case, including outputs and outcomes. |
| 6 | AI Ethics Board — typical line-of-defense placement? | Second line — cross-functional oversight body that reviews ethical risk and approves high-risk use cases; provides challenge, not daily operations. |
| 7 | RACI: 'Accountable' vs 'Consulted' — key exam distinction? | Accountable = ultimate ownership/sign-off, only ONE per task (e.g., model risk owner). Consulted = provides expert input but does not own the decision (e.g., ethics board). |
| 8 | Board of Directors' role in AI governance oversight? | Sets AI risk appetite, approves AI policy at the strategic level, and receives AI risk reporting/metrics. NOT involved in day-to-day model decisions. |
| 9 | Escalation path for a HIGH severity AI incident — correct sequence? | Model owner (1st line) → Model risk management/AI ethics board (2nd line) → Senior management/governance committee → Board (if material). Severity determines escalation height. |
| 10 | AI policy lifecycle stages — memory trick? | Draft → Review → Approve → Publish → Monitor → Periodic Review/Retire. Exam tip: every policy needs a defined owner and review cadence, not indefinite validity. |
| 11 | Why integrate AI risk into the enterprise ERM framework rather than a siloed framework? | Avoids duplicate/conflicting risk taxonomies, ensures consistent risk appetite application, and enables aggregated enterprise-wide board reporting. Creating a fully separate AI framework is a common WRONG exam answer. |
| 12 | Three Lines of Defense (AI) — how many distinct functional lines exist? | 3 — (1) Operational/model owners, (2) Risk management & compliance oversight including AI ethics board, (3) Internal Audit independent assurance. |
